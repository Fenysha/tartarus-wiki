## Steam Turbine

The **Steam Turbine** is the primary power generation system aboard **Belus-06**, converting the energy of hot water vapor into the electrical power required to operate the train.

The turbine consists of three sequentially connected components: the **inlet compressor, central rotor, and outlet stator**. For normal operation, all three components must be positioned in close proximity and correctly connected.

The system operates on a closed steam cycle. Water is fed into the plasma heater, where it is heated and converted into hot steam. The steam then enters the turbine's inlet compressor and transfers its energy to the central rotor. At the outlet, the spent steam condenses back into the liquid system as cooled water.

> **Important:** The turbine only accepts hot water vapor. Cold or insufficiently heated steam will not be accepted by the inlet compressor.

For a quick startup, the following settings are recommended:

* **Inlet Regulator:** 60–70%.
* **Steam Consumption:** 0.70.
* **Target RPM:** approximately 4500 RPM.

> **Important:** The turbine cannot be shut down normally while the current rotor speed is above **500 RPM**.

### Inlet Regulator

The inlet compressor is equipped with a steam flow regulator.

It determines how quickly the installation attempts to utilize the available steam flow. Lower values reduce steam consumption and potential power output, while higher values allow for greater flow and increased rotor speed.

Excessive steam intake increases the load on the rotor and accelerates its heating.

The regulator should therefore be adjusted gradually while continuously monitoring **RPM, temperature, and system condition**.

### RPM Control

The console allows the operator to set the **target rotor speed**.

The rotor automatically attempts to reach the specified value, but actual RPM depends on the amount and temperature of incoming steam. If the steam supply is insufficient, the rotation speed will begin to decrease regardless of the configured target.

Maximum values should not be used without a specific reason.

Once approximately **90% of maximum RPM** is reached, additional rotor wear begins to accumulate. The further the rotor exceeds the safe operating range, the faster damage accumulates.

### Temperature and Overheating

The Steam Turbine must be kept within a controlled temperature range at all times.

Rotor temperature depends on the temperature of the incoming steam and changes during operation. Exceeding the permitted temperature causes the installation to take damage.

The console will issue a warning if overheating occurs. Continued operation under these conditions will cause damage to accumulate over time.

Maximum RPM must also be monitored alongside temperature. Operating at high RPM causes additional wear even when the temperature remains within the normal range.

> **Warning:** Overheating and excessive RPM can result in rotor failure. If the central rotor reaches critical damage, it is destroyed, causing the subsequent explosion of the installation.

## Electrical Power Output

Power generation is determined by the rotor's current rotation speed and the overall efficiency of the turbine components.

More efficient components can provide greater power output under the same operating conditions.

The installation's efficiency also depends on the turbine modules installed. Upgrading individual components can increase performance and alter the installation's permitted operating characteristics.

During prolonged continuous operation, the turbine gradually becomes more efficient. Every **15 minutes** of uninterrupted operation increases its power output by **10%**.

## Water Recovery

Water is not completely lost after passing through the turbine.

The outlet stator returns the recovered water to the liquid system, allowing a closed cycle to be established:

**Water → Plasma Heater → Hot Steam → Steam Turbine → Cooled Water → Heater.**

For full circulation, liquid pipes must be connected to the stator's outlet and linked to the heater's water supply system.

The closed cycle is not completely lossless, so the water supply must be replenished periodically.

## Emergency Shutdown

The control console provides an **emergency dump** function.

It is intended to rapidly reduce the load on the installation when normal steam regulation is no longer sufficient to safely stop the rotor from accelerating.

The emergency dump should be used when temperature or RPM begins rising critically. It does not, however, repair damage that has already been sustained by the components.

## Operation and Maintenance

During operation, the crew should regularly monitor the turbine's condition, steam intake, rotor temperature, and current RPM.

The turbine should not be operated continuously at its maximum limits in pursuit of peak power output. It is designed for sustained operation at a stable load rather than constant acceleration toward maximum RPM.

If overheating or excessive RPM is detected, the load should be reduced immediately and the installation stabilized.

The turbine's primary components should also be inspected periodically for damage. A damaged rotor is particularly dangerous, as continued operation may result in catastrophic failure.

## Quick Reference

**Hot Steam → Start Turbine → Gradually Increase RPM → Monitor Temperature and Rotor Condition → Return Water to the Circuit → Maintain Safe RPM.**

Whenever overheating or uncontrolled RPM increase is detected, stabilizing the installation takes priority over maximizing power output.
