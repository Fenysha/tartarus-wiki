## General Information

**Khara** is a bioengineered pathogen of extraterrestrial origin that spreads in the form of **Veral khara** spores.

Once inside a host, the spores begin colonizing organic tissue and cause accelerated cellular transformation. During the early stages, the infection may resemble a rapidly developing form of cancer. As the disease progresses, a new biological structure begins to develop within the host.

Khara does not rely on conventional immune mechanisms and can infect an organism regardless of its natural resistance to viral diseases.

## Routes of Infection

Khara spores can enter the body through inhalation of contaminated air or direct contact with infected material.

During the later stages of infection, the host begins releasing **Khara miasma**, which can spread the infection through the surrounding environment.

Infected mutants pose an additional threat. Their attacks can transmit the infection through tissue damage, making direct contact with them highly inadvisable whenever possible.

## Countermeasures

Khara is considered incurable, but its progression can be significantly slowed and, at certain stages, partially reversed.

**Anacea** is the most effective known agent for suppressing the spores. Combined administration of **Technetium-99** enhances its effect.

**Rezadone** and **Haloperidol** can also slow the progression of the infection, although their effectiveness decreases as the disease advances.

## Protection Against Spores

<ImagePanel
  src="trainstation/khara_fog.png"

Khara spores can enter the body through the respiratory tract or through direct contact with infected material.

The **Khara Fog** poses a particular threat. It is a dense form of contaminated environment capable of spreading spores across a considerable area.

Ordinary clothing does not provide sufficient protection against contaminated atmospheres. Personnel operating in infected areas must use respiratory protection.

A combined biological protection rating of **7 BIO or higher** from the suit and mask completely prevents exposure to **Khara Fog**.

Using an internal air supply also prevents infection through inhalation of the surrounding atmosphere.

However, respiratory protection does not provide complete protection against direct contact between spores and the body. Preventing this form of infection requires a higher level of biological protection.

## Khara Mutants

Mutants are organisms formed through the further development of Khara.

Most known forms possess considerable physical resilience and can regenerate damage if they are not subjected to further injury for a period of time.

Mutants retain the ability to transmit Khara through attacks and may remain dangerous even after sustaining severe damage.

A common vulnerability among many forms is electrical interference. A sufficiently powerful electrical discharge can temporarily incapacitate a mutant.

Some forms possess high resistance to weaker weapons. Weapons that inflict insufficient damage may have little to no effect on them. More powerful melee weapons, on the other hand, can inflict significantly greater damage.

## Combat Recommendations

Engaging Khara mutants in close combat without suitable weapons is not recommended. Specialized anti-Khara weaponry can effectively eliminate certain mutant forms at close range.

The primary objective during an encounter is to maintain continuous pressure on the target and prevent it from beginning to regenerate. Electrical shocks can be used to temporarily incapacitate particularly dangerous targets.

Avoid contact with blood and other biological remains left by mutants. If the skin has open wounds, such contact presents an additional risk of infection.

## Post-Mortem Mutation

The death of an infected individual does not always mean that the threat has ended.

If the infection has reached a sufficiently advanced stage, Khara continues developing within the corpse. After some time, the formation of a new organism begins.

For this reason, the bodies of patients who died from severe infection should not be left unattended.

A deceased infected individual should be treated as a potential source of further Khara propagation until their condition has been confirmed safe.
